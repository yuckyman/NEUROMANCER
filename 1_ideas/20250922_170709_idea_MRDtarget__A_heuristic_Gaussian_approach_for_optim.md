by Xuwen Wang, Yanfang Guan, Wei Gao, Xin Lai, Wuqiang Cao, Xiaoyan Zhu, Xiaoling Zeng, Yuqian Liu, Shenjie Wang, Ruoyu Liu, Xin Yi, Shuanying Yang, Jiayin Wang Molecular residual disease (MRD) detection, initially developed for hematologic malignancies, has become a critical biomarker for monitoring solid tumors. MRD detection primarily relies on circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) analysis using next-generation sequencing, offering high sensitivity and broad genomic coverage. However, challenges remain in designing cost-effective panels that maximize mutation detection while maintaining biological relevance. Fixed panels often lack sufficient patient-specific mutation coverage, while WES-based personalized MRD assays, despite their high sensitivity, are costly and less accessible.
