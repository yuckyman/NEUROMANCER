summary: 'The article discusses the challenges faced by data-driven decision support in high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance, such as regulatory, institutional, and privacy concerns. While recent generative AI models have shown impressive performance in open-domain tasks, their adoption in sensitive environments remains limited due to unpredictable behaviors and insufficient privacy-preserving datasets for benchmarking.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a comprehensive evaluation framework with standardized utility and fidelity metrics, encompassing nine curated datasets that capture various aspects of healthcare and finance. The key contributions include:

1. A novel evaluation method: The authors introduce a new evaluation metric called "differential privacy" (DP) to enable the generation of synthetic data with formal privacy assurances.

2. A comprehensive dataset selection process: They propose a standardized approach for selecting datasets that are suitable for their evaluation framework, ensuring they capture relevant aspects of healthcare and finance while maintaining privacy.

3. An anonymization method: The authors present a novel approach to anonymize unstructured text data using differential privacy techniques, which can help in mitigating the risks associated with redaction and masking.

4. A detailed methodology: The article provides a detailed description of their evaluation process and methods used throughout the paper.

5. A practical application: The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their proposed approach through a case study involving healthcare data, showcasing how it addresses the challenges faced by decision support systems in sensitive environments.'
