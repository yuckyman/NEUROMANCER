title: Exogenous spatial attention is functional in paralytic strabismics
link: https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2025.1650468
summary: 'The article discusses the absence of exogenous cueing effects in patients with paralytic strabismus, which has been interpreted as evidence for an obligate linkage between movement execution and attentional allocation. The study challenges this interpretation through two experiments that measured the impact of visual cues on response latencies. By including a no-cue control condition, the researchers assessed both the presence and direction of cue-induced attentional modulation.

The results show that all patients' patterns of saccadic (Experiment I) and keypress (Experiment II) response latencies showed classic signatures of cue-target interactions: movements initiated in response to targets spatially proximate to cues were initiated fastest, whereas those initiated toward targets spatially distal from cues were initiated slowest. When grouping latencies by cue-saccade offset to account for differences in target distance, the authors found that these patterns remained consistent across patients with and without exogenous cueing effects.

The article concludes that the absence of exogenous cueing effects in patients with paralytic strabismus supports an obligate linkage between movement execution and attentional allocation.'
