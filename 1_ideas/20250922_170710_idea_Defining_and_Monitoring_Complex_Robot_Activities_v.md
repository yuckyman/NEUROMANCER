It highlights how these LLMs can be used to automate complex tasks involving multiple atomic tasks that may vary depending on the situation. The authors propose a general architecture that combines LLMs with automated planning, allowing humans to specify high-level activities using natural language and monitor their execution.

Key points:

1. LLMs are integrated with autonomous robots in dynamic environments.
2. These LLMs can automate complex tasks involving multiple atomic tasks.
3. They enable humans to specify high-level activities (e.g., processes) using natural language.
4. The architecture combines LLMs with automated planning, enabling humans to monitor the progress of these high-level activities.

The main contribution of this work is a novel approach that bridges the gap between human expertise and machine learning capabilities in autonomous robotics.'

arXiv:2509.16006v1 Announce Type: cross Abstract: Recent years have witnessed a growing interest in automating labor-intensive and complex activities, i.e., those consisting of multiple atomic tasks, by deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable environments such as industrial and agricultural settings. A key characteristic of these contexts is that activities are not predefined: while they involve a limited set of possible tasks, their combinations may vary depending on the situation. Moreover, despite recent advances in robotics, the ability for humans to monitor the progress of high-level activities - in terms of past, present, and future actions - remains fundamental to ensure the correct execution of safety-critical processes.
