# Bayesian Approaches to Face-to-Face Communication

## summary
This article discusses Bayesian approaches that aim to optimize the seamless integration of multisensory signals during face-to-face communication, including voice, gaze, and head movements. These methods are critical for understanding fundamental computational challenges in face-to-face communication.

Prior expectations guide multisensory integration during face-to-face communication

by Giulia Mazzi, Ambra Ferrari, Maria Laura Mencaroni, Chiara Valzolgher, Mirko Tommasini, Francesco Pavani, Stefania Benetti Face-to-face communication relies on the seamless integration of multisensory signals, including voice, gaze, and head movements, to convey meaning effectively. This poses a fundamental computational challenge: optimally binding signals sharing the same communicative intention (e.g., looking at the addressee while speaking) and segregating unrelated signals (e.g., looking away while coughing), all within the rapid turn-taking dynamics of conversation. Critically, the computational mechanisms underlying this extraordinary feat remain largely unknown. Here, we cast face-to-face communication as a Bayesian Causal Inference problem to formally test whether prior expectations arbitrate between the integration and segregation of vocal and bodily signals.
