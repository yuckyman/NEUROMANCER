summary: 'The article discusses a study that investigates the impact of the measles vaccination programme on the current prevalence of measles in Africa. The researchers developed a mathematical model using information from regular serological surveys in Kilifi Health and Demographic Surveillance System (KHDSS) combined with birth cohort data to estimate the relative contribution of the vaccination program to current measles immunity.

The study found that while the vaccination coverage was high, it did not significantly reduce the prevalence of measles. Instead, it contributed to a higher proportion of children who are either measles-naive or seroconverted due to natural infection or vaccination through first dose MCV1, second dose MCV2, or supplementary immunisation activities (SIAs). The model also showed that the coverage rate for the third dose MCV3 was lower than expected, which may indicate that some children might have been vaccinated but were not yet fully protected against measles.'
