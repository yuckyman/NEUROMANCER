summary: 'The article discusses how the temporal integration in the human auditory cortex is primarily affected by absolute time rather than by the duration of speech structures like phonemes or words. The study, published in Nature Neuroscience on September 18, 2025, explores the phenomenon of temporal integration within the auditory cortex and its relationship to absolute time.

The researchers found that during speech processing, the temporal integration is predominantly locked to absolute time rather than varying with the duration of speech structures like phonemes or words. This suggests a more fixed and stable temporal organization in the auditory cortex compared to other brain regions involved in speech perception.

The study highlights several key points:
1. Temporal integration: The human auditory cortex exhibits this phenomenon, where the processing of sound is primarily influenced by absolute time rather than duration.
2. Duration effects: Previous research has shown that the temporal dynamics of speech are affected by the duration of phonemes and words, but not necessarily by the duration of speech structures themselves.
3. Temporal organization: The study suggests a more stable temporal organization in the auditory cortex compared to other brain regions involved in speech processing.

These findings have implications for understanding how the human auditory system processes speech signals and may lead to insights into disorders like dyslexia, where there is often an issue with phoneme recognition or word segmentation.'
