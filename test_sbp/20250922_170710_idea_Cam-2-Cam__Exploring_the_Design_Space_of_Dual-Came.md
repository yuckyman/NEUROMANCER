summary: 'The article discusses a new type of smartphone-based augmented reality system called Cam-2-Cam. This system allows users to interact with the augmented reality environment through both front-facing and rear-facing cameras, rather than relying on a single camera. The authors present two design lessons that explore the interaction space of smartphone AR while maintaining critical AR interface attributes like embodiment and immersion.

The first lesson is about balancing contextual relevance and feedback quality. They found that implementing familiar interactions people do in their real world can help maintain these qualities in augmented reality applications, but they also emphasize the importance of avoiding disorientation during AR interactivity.

The second lesson focuses on preventing disorientation using simultaneous capture and alternating cameras. This approach helps users to navigate through the augmented reality environment without losing their focus or orientation.'
